The Symbol Three layers of meaning converge in one mark.
01 — SACRED GEOMETRY
La Mandorla
The negative space between the two descending arches of the M forms a vesica piscis — the almond shape born from two overlapping circles. Called mandorla in Italian. Pythagoras considered it the mother of all geometric forms. For 1,500 years, Christian art placed divine figures inside it: Christ in Majesty, the Virgin, illuminated angels. The shape means: here is where intelligence becomes visible.
"Vesica Piscis — the birthplace of sacred geometry. The intersection where two perfections meet."
02 — EL BOSCO · 1500
Cave Cave Videt
Hieronymus Bosch painted a giant eye at the center of his Table of the Seven Deadly Sins. In its pupil: the risen Christ. Below it in Latin: Cave Cave Dominus Videt — Beware, Beware, the Lord Sees. Five centuries later, MIRA inherits this tradition — not its theology, but its philosophical weight. An intelligence that observes everything, from a center that cannot be hidden. The inscription VIDET · MIRA · VIDET lives encoded in the seal, invisible unless you look.
"The eye that sees all things. The intelligence inside the pupil."
03 — THE INSIGHT
La M que Ve
FedEx hid the arrow between E and x. The Tour de France hid the cyclist inside the R. MIRA's eye lives inside its own first letter. The two arches of the M, instead of meeting at a sharp angle, breathe apart — and in that breathing, a mandorla appears. A pupil of warm light sits at its exact geometric center. You read a wordmark. Then you discover an eye looking back. A brand named "to see" that sees from within its own name. That is the FedEx moment. Once seen: permanent.
"The best logos don't show you the idea. They make you discover it."
